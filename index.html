01 Rotary boring

Every pile starts with a hole.

A pile is a long concrete column cast deep into the ground. It carries the weight of a building down to firm soil or rock, so the structure above never settles, tilts or cracks.

  1. 1The rig sets up on the pile mark.
  2. 2The rotary head turns the tool down through each layer of ground.
  3. 3Every run comes up full. The spoil is spun off and the tool goes back down, until the design depth.

02 Reinforcement cage and concrete

Steel goes in. Concrete comes up.

The steel cage is lowered into the open bore. Then concrete is placed through a tremie pipe that reaches the very bottom, so the pile fills from below and the pipe is drawn up as the concrete rises.

  1. 1A crawler crane lowers the cage, which holds the steel exactly where the design wants it.
  2. 2The tremie pipe is picked up and lowered to the bottom of the bore.
  3. 3Ready-mix concrete runs down the pipe and fills the pile from the bottom up.

03 Static load testing

Then we prove it.

Before a building is loaded onto a pile, we load the pile. A jack pushes against a frame stacked with weights, and gauges read how far the pile head moves at every step. That curve shows the pile will carry its design load.

  1. 1A crane stacks the kentledge, the weights the jack will push against.
  2. 2The jack loads the pile in steps up to 1.5 times the design load. The gauges read the settlement.
  3. 3The engineer reads the load-settlement curve.
